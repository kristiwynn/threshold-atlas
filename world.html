Walk the threshold

A world built from the numbers

A journey in ten stages, each one a feature people report, in the order the research most often finds them. How strongly each stage appears is set by the share of people in a study who reported it. Choose whose experience to walk through.

ModeEvery stage plays, each as strong as its share of people.

Enter the passage

About three minutes. You move forward through the stages toward a light and back. Drag or swipe to look around. Sound is off until you turn it on, nothing flashes, and you can pause or leave at any time.

In the typical path, a stage reported by fewer people appears fainter and passes sooner. In one person's walk, each stage is included or skipped at random by its share of people, so every walk is different and, over many walks, the stages appear as often as the study found them. A share of walks is distressing, by the study's own rate. Plays in any phone or laptop browser; headset mode (WebXR) is planned.

Sound

What people say they heard

Turn sound on inside the scene and each stage carries what experiencers describe. Nothing here is a recording; every sound is built live in the browser from the descriptions below. Headphones help.

Why does it look like this?

Every stage has a source

The scene reads one file, scene-params.json, and nothing else. The order of stages follows Kenneth Ring's five-stage model (peace, leaving the body, darkness, light, entering the light) and the Liège analysis of 154 narratives, which found leaving the body most often comes first and returning last, with the middle varying from person to person.
