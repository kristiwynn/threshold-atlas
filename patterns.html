Patterns

What the experiences contain

Researchers count features with a standard checklist, the 16-item Greyson NDE Scale. Numbers differ between studies because the samples differ: hospital patients interviewed days after cardiac arrest, versus people who chose to write their experience down years later.

Same feature, different studies

Pick a feature to compare it across the datasets that measured it. Brackets are 95% confidence intervals.

Hospital studies find lower rates than self-selected surveys for almost every feature. Both are real: a survey only hears from people who wanted to tell the story.

Breakdown

Age, sex, culture, and what brought people to the edge

AgeYounger people report them more often.
The evidence

In the Dutch prospective study 52% of those who had an NDE were under 60, against 34% of those who did not (p=0.012). Younger age also predicted an NDE in Greyson's cardiac-unit study. In Morse's Seattle series, critically ill children described NDE features; seriously ill children who had not come near death did not.

SexSimilar rates, possibly different depth.
The evidence

Van Lommel found women more often had deep experiences (p=0.011). A later analysis of the Greyson scale found no overall difference in incidence by sex.

CauseCardiac arrest is the best studied, not the only one.
The evidence

It can be timed, which is why it dominates the literature. Accounts also come from drowning, trauma, childbirth, surgery and illness. Researchers find that once an experience happens, its cause does not seem to change what is in it.

CultureMeeting beings and another world hold up everywhere. The tunnel does not.
The evidence

The tunnel is largely a Western motif. In Pasricha and Stevenson's Indian cases people were taken by messengers to a clerk with a ledger, told a mistake had been made, and sent back. Tibetan deloks have their deeds weighed in public, in a mirror or read aloud, rather than replaying a life privately. The life review is culture- and era-sensitive; being met by others is not.

OrderThere is no fixed sequence.
The evidence

In the Liege analysis of 154 narratives, the famous out-of-body, tunnel, light, peace order appeared in only 6 of the 27 accounts that contained all four features.

EraThe tunnel may be partly learned.
The evidence

Athappilly, Greyson and Stevenson (2006) compared 24 accounts collected before Moody's 1975 book with 24 collected after. A tunnel appeared in 16% of the earlier accounts and 64% of the later ones: the only one of Moody's 15 features that changed.

ToneMost are described as positive.
The evidence

In the NDERF survey 62.5% called theirs wonderful, 33.8% mixed and 3.8% frightening. In 123 Liege narratives, 17 were distressing.

What a Greyson score means

Sixteen questions, scored 0, 1 or 2, out of 32. Seven or more counts as an NDE.

What the threshold does, and what it misses

Sixteen questions, each scored 0, 1 or 2, for a total out of 32. A score of 7 or more is the usual threshold for calling an experience an NDE. The items fall in four groups:

  • Cognitive: time speeding or slowing, thoughts speeding up, life review, sudden understanding
  • Affective: peace, joy, unity with the universe, a brilliant light
  • Paranormal: vivid senses, awareness of distant events, scenes from the future, out-of-body
  • Transcendental: another world, a mystical presence, deceased or religious figures, a border

"Unity with the universe" and "scenes from the future" are already items 7 and 11, so this atlas tracks them with the standard scale rather than as extra questions.
