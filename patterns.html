Patterns

What the experiences contain

Researchers count features with a standard checklist, the 16-item Greyson NDE Scale. Numbers differ between studies because the samples differ: hospital patients interviewed days after cardiac arrest, versus people who chose to write their experience down years later.

Same feature, different studies

Pick a feature to compare it across the datasets that measured it. Brackets are 95% confidence intervals.

Hospital studies find lower rates for almost every feature than self-selected surveys. Both are real; they count different populations, and a survey only hears from people who wanted to tell the story.

Breakdown

Age, sex, culture, and what brought people to the edge

AgeIn the Dutch prospective study, 52% of the people who had an NDE were under 60, against 34% of those who did not (p=0.012), and younger age also predicted an NDE in Greyson's cardiac-unit study. Children report them too: in Morse's Seattle series, critically ill children described NDE features while seriously ill children who had not come near death did not.
SexMen and women report NDEs at similar rates. Van Lommel found women more often had deep experiences (p=0.011); a later analysis of the Greyson scale across many samples found depth unrelated to sex or age (Lange, Greyson & Houran 2004).
CauseCardiac arrest is the best-studied trigger because it can be timed. Accounts also come from drowning, trauma, childbirth, surgery and illness. Once an NDE happens, what caused the crisis does not seem to change its content; in the Dutch study, how long the heart stopped, the drugs given and prior fear of death did not predict who had one.
CultureMeeting beings and entering another world are the features that hold up across cultures. The tunnel is largely a Western motif. In Pasricha and Stevenson's Indian cases, people were taken by messengers to a clerk with a ledger, told a mistake had been made, and sent back. The life review is also culture- and era-sensitive.
OrderThere is no fixed sequence. In the Liège analysis of 154 narratives, the famous out-of-body, tunnel, light, peace order appeared in only 6 of the 27 accounts that contained all four features.
EraThe tunnel may be partly learned. Athappilly, Greyson and Stevenson (2006) compared 24 accounts collected before Moody's 1975 book with 24 collected after: a tunnel appeared in 16% of the earlier accounts and 64% of the later ones, the only one of Moody's 15 features that changed.
ToneMost experiences are described as positive. In the NDERF survey, 62.5% called theirs wonderful, 33.8% mixed and 3.8% frightening. In 123 Liège narratives, 14% were distressing (Cassol 2019).

What a Greyson score means

Sixteen questions, each scored 0, 1 or 2, for a total out of 32. A score of 7 or more is the usual threshold for calling an experience an NDE. The items fall in four groups:

  • Cognitive: time speeding or slowing, thoughts speeding up, life review, sudden understanding
  • Affective: peace, joy, unity with the universe, a brilliant light
  • Paranormal: vivid senses, awareness of distant events, scenes from the future, out-of-body
  • Transcendental: another world, a mystical presence, deceased or religious figures, a border

"Unity with the universe" and "scenes from the future" are already items 7 and 11, so this atlas tracks them with the standard scale rather than as extra questions.
